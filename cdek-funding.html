The gap CDEK fills

By the time a medicine reaches a patient, the molecule has carried several names: an industry code during clinical trials, a generic name to distinguish its active ingredient, a proprietary brand name, and often different names in different countries. Public databases don’t connect them fully, so the trials run under the experimental code are invisible to anyone searching by the name on the prescription bottle.

CDEK is built around exactly that link. It is the only public database that maps a drug's experimental names to its final generic and brand names, curated from ChEMBL, DrugBank, PubChem, ClinicalTrials.gov, and the FDA.

Alternatives to CDEK cost tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars a year per seat and are closed to students, independent researchers, patients, and most universities, which don't come close to matching the depth and breadth of the CDEK database. CDEK is free, and the data it curates is public to begin with. The only question is whether someone maintains it.

Fifteen years of work and a close call

Dr. Michael Kinch started CDEK about 15 years ago as a working file to answer his own research questions, while founding the Yale Center for Drug Discovery. No tool showed the entire history of a drug in one place, so he built one that stitched the public sources together. CDEK was hosted by powerful research institutions, including Washington University and Purdue. During the initial building of the database, the progression of the web application, and over several years, the user base grew to roughly 15,000 active users per month.

In the spring of 2026, however, Purdue notified Dr. Kinch that it no longer had the resources to host or fund CDEK. Knowing that Data 4 The People rescues public datasets, Mike asked if we could rescue this incredible database and application. We said yes. After looking at the legacy application, we made the executive decision to rebuild the user-interface and the back-end processes with modern programming architecture. The revamp is a work in progress, but it is live at cdek.data4thepeople.com. With the new infrastructure we can now prevent autonomous bots from scraping the data, users can create a free account with upgraded security features, and it will be easier to maintain and expand its overall impact. We also have the ability to integrate modern features that can help users navigate their research, such as agentic AI.

So, what remains to be done?

  • DATA: it has been frozen since spring 2026, its update process is manual, and depends on one person with expert knowledge. The data also needs to be thoroughly audited to understand how to improve quality and accessibility.
  • AWARENESS: we want more researchers, academics, students, and, eventually, the general public to know what CDEK is and how to use it. Researchers who used it in its legacy form are now being automatically rerouted to the new site, but that doesn’t help build the user-base.

About Data 4 The People

D4TP is a nonprofit data-journalism organization. We rescue publicly available data and build tools and visualizations that help professional and citizen journalists make sense of their communities and local governments. CDEK is the clearest case for that work: a critical research resource months from going offline with the possibility of expanding its reach and value to those who have the power to build meaningful connections where it matters most.
