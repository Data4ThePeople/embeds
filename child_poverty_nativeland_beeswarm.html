Child poverty: school districts on Native land vs. the rest of the US

Related children ages 5–17 in families in poverty, by school district. SAIPE 2024.

Median child poverty is 22.4% in Native-land school districts vs. 12.4% elsewhere — nearly double.

Native-land school districts are individual dots. The rest-of-US distribution is a filled band whose width tracks the number of districts at each poverty rate; its sparse high-poverty tail is shown as dots.

Horizontal width is meaningful: it shows how many districts share a given poverty rate (wider = more). For the rest of the US, the dense core is drawn as a filled band (its width tracks the count at each rate); only the sparse outer districts appear as individual dots. Box = middle 50% (IQR), center line = median, whiskers = range within 1.5×IQR. Hover any reservation dot for its district.
Sources: U.S. Census Bureau SAIPE 2024; NCES EDGE School District Geographic Relationship File 2025 (LEA↔AIANNH crosswalk). "Native land" = federal American Indian reservations, off-reservation trust lands, Alaska Native village areas, state-recognized reservations, and Hawaiian Home Lands; a district counts when ≥75% of its land area falls within them. Oklahoma Tribal Statistical Areas are excluded, as they overlay entire metros (Tulsa, Oklahoma City) rather than governed land bases. Hawaii has no qualifying district: the state is a single school district, so Hawaiian Home Lands never reach the 75% threshold. Box statistics use unweighted district-level rates.
